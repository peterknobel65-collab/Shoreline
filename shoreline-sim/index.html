Laulea Shoreline Lab

A physics model of the eroding beach in front of 461 Laulea Place, built from the 2023 lidar survey, a century of surveyed shorelines, dated aerial photos, and real NOAA wave-buoy and tide records. Use it to compare legal, soft ways to stabilise the shoreline: native dune plants, sand nourishment and coir (coconut-fibre) toe protection. You can also test what large winter swells, king tides and sea-level rise do to each option.

1 · Site reading

What the survey data and photos show

A long-term eroding shoreSurveyed shorelines from 1912 to 2002 put the shoreline in front of 461 about 40 m farther inland today than in 1912. The measured rate is 0.49 m/yr (1912–2002) and 0.64 m/yr (1960–2002). The coast for a kilometre to the west erodes at 0.4–1.0 m/yr.
A low bank, 11 m from the houseThe 2023 lidar puts the bank top at only +2.9 m above sea level at 461, lower than the neighbouring lots (+3.3 to +3.8 m). The bank edge is about 11 m from the roof edge.
Walls on the neighbouring lots459's rock wall has held its line since 1960, while the open beach in front of 461 kept retreating. Walls on both sides make 461 the soft gap in the line.
Waves cut it this fallYour November photos show a fresh 0.5–0.7 m vertical cut, roots and irrigation pipe exposed, and a cobble lag at the toe. The beach has dropped since the 2023 lidar.
Faces north-northwest (350°)Big winter swells from 315–335° arrive nearly head-on, and long-period ones (17–22 s) run up highest. The reef offshore takes some of the energy.
Placed bouldersThe loose basalt boulders on the beach were put there by people without authorisation. They are not part of any permitted protection, and the model gives them no protective value.

4 · Recommendation (draft)

A planted dune on your lawn, then sand on the beach

The bank at 461 is the lowest and most exposed point between two walls, on a coast that has lost about half a metre a year for a century. At that rate, doing nothing brings the bank to the house in roughly 15–20 years, sooner as the sea rises. Plants alone slow it by about a third. Sand fixes the cause, a beach too thin to absorb the swell, but only while it is topped up. Some of your sand will drift in front of 459 and 463; the model accounts for that loss.

  1. This winter: mark two fixed points (a house corner and a post) and tape-measure to the bank edge monthly and after every big swell. Cap and reroute the exposed irrigation pipe. Keep people off the bank face. Do not add rocks or move the placed boulders.
  2. Next 1–3 months: hire a licensed surveyor for a certified shoreline and a beach profile. That fixes where your property ends and replaces the lidar and photo estimates here.
  3. Spring 2027: apply to Maui County Planning for a planted sand dune on your lawn, landward of the certified shoreline. It raises the low +2.9 m bank top, keeps winter overwash off the lawn, and gives you a sand reserve on your own land.
  4. In parallel: apply to DLNR-OCCL for small-scale beach nourishment along your frontage. Start with Category I (under 500 yd³) and ask for a maintenance volume, so top-ups every 3–5 years need no new application.
  5. Summer (calm season): build the dune, place the beach sand against the bank, add coir envelopes in the toe if approved, and plant densely.
  6. Every year: re-measure, replant after big winters, and re-run this model with the new numbers.

Native plants for the dune

  • ʻAkiʻaki (Sporobolus virginicus): low grass, dense roots, binds sand at the front of the dune.
  • Pōhuehue (Ipomoea pes-caprae): already on site; long runners trap blowing sand.
  • Naupaka kahakai (Scaevola taccada): shrub on the dune crest, deep woody roots.
  • Hinahina kū kahakai (Heliotropium anomalum), nanea (Vigna marina), ʻilima (Sida fallax): fill in and hold the surface.

Avoid

  • Adding rocks, boulders, seawalls or revetments. State law prohibits private shoreline hardening where there is a sand beach, and the boulders already there were placed without authorisation.
  • Moving the boulders or pushing beach sand against the bank yourself. The beach is state land; ask OCCL how unauthorised rocks should be dealt with.
  • Planting to push the shoreline seaward. Planted vegetation is not supposed to set the shoreline location.
  • Plastic sandbags or geotextile left long-term. These need a time-limited permit and removal.

6 · Method

How the model works

Waves. Hourly significant wave height, peak period and direction from NOAA buoy 51205 (PacIOOS/CDIP 187), about 9 mi off Pauwela. Waves are refracted toward the shore normal with Snell's law, then cut by a reef transmission factor.

Water level. Measured hourly water level at Kahului Harbor (NOAA 1615680), so seasonal highs, king tides and surge are real. Sea-level rise is added on top.

Runup. Stockdon et al. (2006) 2%-exceedance runup from wave height, period and beach slope. Total water level is tide plus sea-level rise plus runup.

Impact regime. Sallenger (2000): swash (below the bank), collision (hitting the bank), overwash (over the lawn edge).

Erosion. Larson, Erikson & Hanson (2004) wave-impact model, dV/dt = 4·Cs·(TWL − ztoe)²/T. Sand in front of the bank goes first. The cohesive, rooted bank erodes at a calibrated fraction of that rate, reduced further by plant roots. Retreat equals eroded volume divided by bank height.

Sand budget. Sand stripped off the beach moves offshore. Most of it returns in calm weather and over summer; part leaves the pocket for good. That loss is the chronic erosion.

Nourishment spreading. Pelnard-Considère diffusion spreads sand placed on your 34 m frontage along the roughly 110 m of beach between the two walls, so only part of it stays in front of 461.

Site data. Bank and beach heights from the USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar (August 2023). Retreat rate from UH/USGS surveyed shorelines 1912–2002 (transects 598–600). House distance and condition from dated aerial imagery (2016–2026) and your November 2026 photos.

Calibration. The bank's erodibility is tuned so that “do nothing” matches the measured long-term retreat rate at 461, 0.49 m/yr.

Tests: npm test checks the runup formula against a hand calculation, sand conservation, calibration accuracy, and that more protection never produces more retreat.

7 · Open questions

What would sharpen the model next

  1. How far is the bank edge from the house today, measured with a tape from a fixed corner? The 11 m here comes from the January 2026 aerial.
  2. Has the bank moved noticeably since you've owned the house, and in which winters were the big losses?
  3. What is the house's floor height or foundation type? It decides how much overwash the house can take.
  4. What budget and timeline are you working with? Sand volume and how often to top it up drive cost.
